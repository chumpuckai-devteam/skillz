# Strict checklist

Read this only when the user names STE, ASD-STE100, compliance, or strict STE. 80% mode does not use this file.

These are paraphrases of ASD-STE100 Issue 9 habits. They are not the standard's text and they are not a word list. ASD-STE100 is a trademark of ASD. This project is not affiliated with ASD.

## Before drafting

Done when each item below was applied or explicitly marked as a departure.

1. Apply every rung-1 structural rule in `SKILL.md`, including the 20-word procedure cap and the 25-word explanation cap.
2. Procedure notes are information, not instructions. A note may use 25 words. A work step, a warning, and a caution stay at 20.
3. No contractions. Keep articles and "that".
4. American spelling, unless the user or a contract names another spelling.
5. Simple tenses only: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future, and past participle as an adjective. If a perfect tense carries a fact the simple tense loses, keep the perfect tense and mark that line as a departure.
6. Passive voice only in descriptive text, and only when the actor is unknown. "The actor is irrelevant" is not enough. Name the actor, or mark the departure.
7. One name for one thing in the whole document.
8. A domain term may stay as a technical noun or a technical verb. Define it once. Do not use a technical noun as a verb.
9. Prefer one verb over an idiom ("start", not "spin up"). Do not fail a two-word verb without a dictionary. Some multi-word verbs are approved entries.
10. Do not invent approved-word swaps. Without a local word list, lexical choice is a direction, not a check.

## Modals

Apply a mapping only when the meaning matches. Say which mapping you used. If the mapping would overstate uncertain evidence, keep the hedge and mark the departure.

- "should" as a real requirement: "must".
- "should" as a soft recommendation: do not upgrade it when the evidence is uncertain.
- "may" as permission, or as a real chance an event happens: "can".
- "may" as an uncertain fact: "possibly". Do not use "can" for that meaning. "Can" reads as ability or permission.
- "could" as possibility: "can".
- "would": "can", or an if-sentence.

## Dictionary

Do not copy dictionary rows into the reply, this skill, or git.

If the user supplies a local word list built from their own Issue 9 PDF, check verbs against that list. Do not commit the list. A clean check is still not certification.

End the reply with one sentence: this is not certified ASD-STE100 compliance, and the official dictionary was not checked unless a local word list was supplied.
